When a Korean company announces a share buyback, investors often assume the share count will shrink. That only happens if the shares are **cancelled**. Many Korean companies have historically kept repurchased shares as treasury stock instead.

## The filings you will see

| Report name | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 자기주식취득결정 | The board decided to buy back shares directly. |
| 자기주식취득신탁계약체결결정 | The company signed a trust contract with a securities firm, which buys shares on its behalf. |
| 주식소각결정 / 자기주식소각결정 | The board decided to cancel shares. This is what reduces the share count. |
| 자기주식처분결정 | The company decided to sell or hand out treasury shares, for example to employees or in an exchange. |
| 자기주식취득결과보고서 | The result report after the buyback period ends: how many shares were actually bought. |

## Direct buyback vs trust contract

A **direct buyback** states the number of shares and the period. Korean rules require the company to buy within the stated period.

A **trust contract** states an amount of money given to a securities firm. The firm buys at its discretion, and the company is not obliged to spend the full amount. Investors usually see direct buybacks as a firmer commitment.

## Why cancellation matters

Treasury shares carry no voting rights or dividends while held, but they can be sold back into the market or used in mergers and exchanges. A cancellation removes them permanently. For that reason, Korean investors often watch for a cancellation filing after a buyback.

The treatment of treasury shares has been part of Korea's corporate governance reform debate, including the Corporate Value-up Program. Rules may change, so check the latest official guidance.

## What to check in a buyback filing

1. Number of shares and maximum amount.
2. Purpose (취득목적). "주주가치 제고" means raising shareholder value.
3. Period and method — on-market (장내매수) or tender offer (공개매수).
4. Treasury shares already held (취득 전 자기주식 보유현황).
5. Whether a cancellation is mentioned.
